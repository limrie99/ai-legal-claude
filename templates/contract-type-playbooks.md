# Contract-Type Playbooks

Use these playbooks to calibrate contract review, redlines, negotiations, missing-protection checks, and generated agreements. They are not legal advice. They are practical issue maps for deciding what to inspect, what to prioritize, and what fallback language may be commercially reasonable.

## Universal Review Rules

- Identify the reviewer role before scoring risk.
- Treat jurisdiction-specific enforceability as `[VERIFY CURRENT LAW]` unless the user provides current legal confirmation.
- Focus on deal economics, liability allocation, IP ownership, termination rights, confidentiality, dispute path, and operational obligations.
- Prefer balanced language that a reasonable counterparty might accept.
- Separate dealbreaker changes from drafting polish.

## Freelancer / Contractor Agreement

### Expected Clauses
- Scope of work and deliverables
- Payment schedule and late-payment rights
- Change request process
- Acceptance criteria and revision limits
- Kill fee or cancellation fee
- IP ownership and timing of transfer
- Portfolio rights
- Independent contractor status
- Confidentiality
- Termination
- Liability cap
- Dispute resolution

### Dealbreakers
- IP transfers before full payment.
- Unlimited indemnity for all claims related to the services.
- Broad non-compete or exclusivity.
- No payment deadline or vague acceptance process.
- Client can terminate at convenience without paying completed work or kill fee.

### Strong Fallbacks
- IP transfers only after all undisputed invoices are paid.
- Liability cap at fees paid or payable under the agreement.
- Revisions limited to a defined number of rounds.
- Client feedback due within a fixed period, then deliverables deemed accepted.
- Portfolio use allowed after public launch unless confidentiality requires otherwise.

## SaaS MSA / Software License

### Expected Clauses
- Subscription scope and authorized users
- Service levels / uptime
- Data ownership and data export
- Security controls
- DPA or privacy terms where personal data is processed
- Support obligations
- Fees, renewals, and price increases
- Suspension rights
- IP ownership and license restrictions
- Warranty disclaimer
- Liability cap and carve-outs
- Indemnity for IP infringement
- Termination and transition assistance

### Dealbreakers
- Vendor can change material terms by posting updated policies without notice.
- No data return/export right at termination.
- Broad liability cap carve-outs that swallow the cap.
- Auto-renewal with a very short cancellation window.
- Customer data license allows unrelated product training, resale, or advertising use without consent.

### Strong Fallbacks
- 30 to 60 days notice for material term or price changes.
- Data export for at least 30 days after termination.
- Liability cap at 12 months of fees, with narrow super-cap for confidentiality, data breach, or IP claims.
- Mutual confidentiality and security obligations.
- Cure period before suspension except for urgent security risk.

## NDA

### Expected Clauses
- Clear purpose
- Mutual or one-way structure
- Tailored confidential information definition
- Standard exclusions
- Permitted disclosures to advisors and personnel
- Compelled disclosure process
- Use restriction
- Care standard
- Return or destruction
- Term and survival
- No license
- Equitable remedies
- Governing law

### Dealbreakers
- Confidential information definition covers everything forever without exclusions.
- No compelled-disclosure notice carve-out.
- One-way NDA where both parties will disclose sensitive information.
- Survival period is indefinite for ordinary business information.
- NDA restricts independently developed or already-known information.

### Strong Fallbacks
- Separate trade secrets from ordinary confidential information.
- 2 to 5 year survival for business information; trade secrets protected as long as they remain trade secrets.
- Representatives may receive information if bound by confidentiality duties.
- Return/destruction certification upon request.

## Employment Offer / Employment Agreement

### Expected Clauses
- Role, title, reporting line
- Compensation and bonus terms
- Equity terms or plan references
- Benefits
- At-will status or term
- Confidentiality
- IP assignment
- Restrictive covenants
- Severance, if any
- Dispute resolution
- Governing law

### Dealbreakers
- Non-compete that may be banned or heavily restricted in the worker's state.
- IP assignment captures pre-existing inventions or unrelated side projects.
- Bonus described as discretionary when user expects guaranteed compensation.
- Relocation or repayment obligations without clear limits.
- Arbitration or fee-shifting terms that materially burden the employee.

### Strong Fallbacks
- Exclude pre-existing IP in a schedule.
- Limit inventions assignment to work related to company business or using company resources.
- Convert vague bonus promises into objective targets.
- Add severance triggers for without-cause termination or material role reduction.
- Mark non-compete issues `[VERIFY CURRENT LAW]`.

## Vendor / Services Agreement

### Expected Clauses
- Services and service levels
- Deliverables and acceptance
- Fees, invoicing, and taxes
- Customer responsibilities
- Change orders
- Confidentiality
- Data protection, if applicable
- IP ownership and licenses
- Warranties
- Indemnity
- Liability cap
- Insurance
- Termination and transition
- Audit rights, if applicable

### Dealbreakers
- Vendor indemnifies customer for customer-caused issues.
- Customer can withhold payment for unrelated disputes.
- Acceptance criteria are subjective or undefined.
- No change-order process for out-of-scope work.
- Liability is uncapped despite low contract value.

### Strong Fallbacks
- Objective acceptance criteria and deemed acceptance after a review period.
- Payment of undisputed amounts required even during disputes.
- Mutual indemnity tied to each party's breach, negligence, willful misconduct, or IP infringement.
- Liability cap tied to fees, with narrow exclusions.

## Lease

### Expected Clauses
- Premises description
- Term and renewal rights
- Rent, deposits, fees, and escalations
- Maintenance and repair allocation
- Use restrictions
- Insurance
- Assignment and sublease
- Default and cure periods
- Early termination
- Surrender obligations
- Casualty and condemnation
- Governing law

### Dealbreakers
- Tenant responsible for structural repairs or pre-existing defects.
- Landlord can relocate tenant without meaningful protections.
- Broad personal guarantee.
- Hidden operating expenses with no audit right.
- No cure period before default remedies.

### Strong Fallbacks
- Cap controllable operating expense increases.
- Exclude structural, roof, foundation, and pre-existing condition repairs from tenant obligations.
- Add audit rights for CAM or operating expenses.
- Add reasonable cure periods and notice.
- Limit personal guarantee by amount or burn-off period.

## Partnership / Joint Venture

### Expected Clauses
- Purpose and business scope
- Contributions
- Ownership percentages
- Profit and loss allocation
- Decision rights and reserved matters
- Deadlock process
- Roles and duties
- IP ownership
- Confidentiality
- Transfer restrictions
- Buy-sell rights
- Dissolution and wind-down
- Dispute resolution

### Dealbreakers
- No deadlock process for 50/50 control.
- One partner can bind the venture to major obligations alone.
- Contributions are unequal but economics are unclear.
- No exit, buyout, or dissolution mechanism.
- IP ownership is ambiguous.

### Strong Fallbacks
- Reserved matters require unanimous or supermajority approval.
- Add deadlock escalation, mediation, buy-sell, or shotgun mechanism.
- Document contributions and ownership separately.
- Define ownership of pre-existing IP and jointly developed IP.
- Add non-circumvention or opportunity-allocation language only where reasonable.
