## Clause-by-Clause Analysis

### :red_circle: HIGH RISK CLAUSES

#### Unlimited Revisions — Section 1.3
- **What it says:** Contractor must provide unlimited revisions at no extra cost until the Client declares full satisfaction. No cap on revision cycles.
- **Why it's risky:** Combined with the fixed $15,000 fee, this creates an infinite work obligation. The Client can demand endless revisions and never declare satisfaction, trapping the Contractor in perpetual unpaid work.
- **What you could lose:** $50,000-$150,000+ in uncompensated labor over time.
- **Recommended change:** Cap at 2 revision rounds per milestone. Additional rounds billed at $150/hour. Define "revision" vs. new work. Add 5-business-day acceptance window with deemed-acceptance if Client doesn't respond.

#### Client Can Change Specs Unilaterally — Sections 1.2 + 11.2
- **What it says:** Client can modify the project specifications (Exhibit A) at any time, in its sole discretion, without Contractor consent or additional compensation.
- **Why it's risky:** This is scope creep with contractual authorization. The Client can transform a website redesign into an entirely different project while the price stays at $15,000.
- **What you could lose:** Tens of thousands in uncompensated labor if scope expands significantly.
- **Recommended change:** Require mutual written Change Orders for scope modifications, with cost/timeline impact assessment. No unilateral changes.

#### 90-Day Payment Terms + Subjective Withholding — Section 2.2
- **What it says:** Payment due within 90 days of invoice. Client can withhold payment for work it deems "unsatisfactory" with no objective standard.
- **Why it's risky:** Industry standard is Net 30. Combined with unlimited revisions, the Client can perpetually withhold payment by never declaring satisfaction. The Contractor finances the project for 3 months at a time.
- **What you could lose:** Full $15,000 contract value; severe cash flow impact.
- **Recommended change:** Net 30 payment terms. 25% non-refundable deposit upfront. 10-business-day acceptance window with specific written objections required. 1.5%/month late fee. Right to suspend work if invoice unpaid after 45 days.

#### Pre-Existing IP Assignment — Section 3.2
- **What it says:** The IP assignment includes the Contractor's pre-existing tools, frameworks, libraries, code snippets, templates, and methodologies used in the project.
- **Why it's risky:** This is the most damaging clause in the contract. The Contractor would lose ownership of their entire professional toolkit — code libraries, frameworks, and templates developed over years. The Client would own them outright, and the Contractor would need permission to use their own tools on future projects.
- **What you could lose:** $50,000-$500,000+ in IP value; potentially the Contractor's entire business.
- **Recommended change:** Assign only custom work product created specifically for this project. Contractor retains pre-existing IP with a non-exclusive license to Client to use it as embedded in the delivered work. Condition IP assignment on full payment.

#### No Portfolio Use — Section 4.3
- **What it says:** Contractor cannot disclose the agreement exists, cannot use any work in their portfolio without Client's written consent (which may be withheld in sole discretion).
- **Why it's risky:** Portfolio pieces are essential for winning future freelance work. This eliminates the non-monetary value of the engagement and prevents the Contractor from even listing the Client as a reference.
- **What you could lose:** $10,000-$30,000 in lost future business development value.
- **Recommended change:** Permit portfolio use of final deliverables (excluding Confidential Information). Allow listing Client as a client reference. Offer optional 90-day delay period for Client to announce first.

#### 24-Month Non-Compete Across All Tech — Section 5.1
- **What it says:** For 24 months after termination, Contractor cannot work in any competitive business in the technology industry.
- **Why it's risky:** This is a de facto ban on earning a living. The "technology industry" is undefined and could encompass virtually every modern business. Almost certainly unenforceable in most U.S. jurisdictions (void in CA, CO, MN, OK, ND) but creates litigation risk and chilling effects. The FTC has also moved to restrict non-competes.
- **What you could lose:** 2 years of income ($160,000+) plus $30,000-$80,000 in legal fees to challenge.
- **Recommended change:** Delete the non-compete entirely. If Client insists, narrow to a 6-month non-solicitation of Client's direct customers only, with a paid restriction fee.

#### Immediate Termination With No Pay — Sections 6.3 + 6.5
- **What it says:** Client can terminate at any time, for any reason, effective immediately, with no obligation to pay for work in progress or previously completed but unapproved work.
- **Why it's risky:** The Client could have the Contractor complete 95% of the project, terminate, keep all work product (per Section 3), and owe nothing. This likely constitutes unjust enrichment.
- **What you could lose:** Up to $12,000 in completed but unpaid work, plus all IP transferred.
- **Recommended change:** Require 15 days' notice for termination without cause. Payment for all completed milestones plus pro-rata share of work in progress. Condition IP assignment on full payment.

#### Asymmetric Termination — Sections 6.3 vs 6.4
- **What it says:** Client can terminate instantly. Contractor must give 90 days' notice and continue working during that entire period.
- **Why it's risky:** If the relationship becomes hostile, the Contractor is forced to continue performing for 3 months with no guarantee of payment.
- **Recommended change:** Mutual 15-day notice period. Add termination-for-cause provisions allowing immediate exit if the other party materially breaches.

#### Auto-Renewal Trap — Section 6.2
- **What it says:** Contract auto-renews for 12-month periods unless 120 days' notice of non-renewal is given.
- **Why it's risky:** 120 days (4 months) is an unusually long cancellation window. Miss it by one day and you're locked in for another year. For a project-based engagement, auto-renewal makes no sense.
- **Recommended change:** Remove auto-renewal entirely. Contract ends upon project completion. If ongoing services are needed, month-to-month renewal with 30-day notice.

#### Indemnification for Client's Own Negligence — Section 7.1
- **What it says:** Contractor indemnifies Client for all claims, including claims arising from the Client's own negligence. One-way, with no reciprocal indemnification. Survives indefinitely.
- **Why it's risky:** The Contractor is effectively the Client's insurer. If the Client provides infringing content and gets sued, the Contractor pays. Many jurisdictions void indemnification for one's own negligence.
- **What you could lose:** Unlimited — a single claim could exceed $100,000-$1,000,000+.
- **Recommended change:** Make indemnification mutual. Exclude claims arising from the other party's negligence or breach. Add carve-out for Client-provided materials. Limit survival to 2-3 years.

#### Client Liability Cap = Effectively $0 — Section 8.2
- **What it says:** Client's total liability capped at fees paid in the prior 30 days.
- **Why it's risky:** On a milestone project with 90-day payment terms, the Client may have paid $0 in the preceding 30 days. This is not a liability cap — it is immunity.
- **Recommended change:** Cap both parties' liability at total contract value ($15,000). Exclude liability cap for breaches of confidentiality, indemnification, or gross negligence/willful misconduct.

#### Unlimited Contractor Liability — Section 8.3
- **What it says:** The liability cap applies only to the Client. Contractor has unlimited liability.
- **Why it's risky:** The Contractor's entire personal net worth is at risk on a $15,000 contract. Combined with indemnification for Client's negligence, exposure is theoretically infinite.
- **What you could lose:** Everything — personal and business assets with no ceiling.
- **Recommended change:** Apply the same liability cap to both parties. Cap Contractor liability at total contract value ($15,000) except for fraud, gross negligence, or willful misconduct.

#### BVI Governing Law & Courts — Sections 10.1 + 10.2
- **What it says:** Governed by British Virgin Islands law with exclusive BVI court jurisdiction. Neither party nor the work has any connection to BVI.
- **Why it's risky:** Litigating in the BVI would cost $50,000+ — far exceeding the contract value. This effectively prevents the Contractor from ever bringing a claim. Courts may view this as regulatory evasion by a Delaware LLC to avoid U.S. contractor protections.
- **Recommended change:** Govern by Delaware law (where Client is incorporated) or Contractor's home state. Use AAA arbitration in a mutually convenient U.S. location, with a small-claims court exception.

2. **Cap revisions and add a change order process (Sections 1.2, 1.3, 11.2)** — 2 rounds per milestone included; additional rounds billed hourly. Scope changes require mutual written agreement with cost adjustment.

4. **Remove or drastically narrow non-compete (Section 5.1)** — Delete entirely, or limit to 6-month non-solicitation of named direct competitors with paid restriction fee.

5. **Require payment for completed work on termination (Sections 6.3, 6.5)** — Pro-rata payment for work performed through termination date. 15-day notice for termination without cause.

6. **Make liability mutual and reasonable (Sections 7, 8)** — Mutual indemnification. Both parties capped at total contract value. Exclude indemnification for the other party's negligence.

7. **Change governing law to U.S. jurisdiction (Section 10)** — Delaware or Contractor's home state. AAA arbitration with small-claims exception.

8. **Allow portfolio use (Section 4.3)** — Permit display of final deliverables in portfolio; allow listing Client as reference.

9. **Fix termination asymmetry (Section 6.3 vs 6.4)** — Mutual 15-day notice. Add termination-for-cause provisions for both parties.
